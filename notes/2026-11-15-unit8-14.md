## 8. Design Tradeoffs

- Speed, area, power, and throughput cannot all be optimal at once. The same function can be a deep combinational block (fast, large) or split into stages (slower, smaller).
- In RTL, **where you cut a pipeline is the latency/throughput trade**. It feeds directly into the W5 area work and into answering "why this structure?" in an interview.
- Question: if you replace the 4-bit ripple adder with a carry-lookahead adder, what improves and what gets worse?

## 9. Designing an Instruction Set

- An ISA is a design problem about how to allocate bits: opcode, register numbers, immediate. Fixed-length instructions simplify decoding but cost immediate width.
- In RTL, **the instruction decoder is just bit-field slicing**. Conditions like `opcode == 6'b100000` are products of the convention you chose here.
- Question: with 32-bit fixed length, 16 registers, and a 6-bit opcode, how many bits remain for the immediate at most?

## 10. Assembly Language, Models of Computation

- Assembly is the human-readable form of an ISA, and at the same time a conversation about abstraction: is this an FSM, or a machine with state?
- In RTL, being able to say **what machine this circuit computes** decides the quality of the spec. In verification it justifies "which model computes the expected value".
- Question: is `adder4` a combinational circuit or a model of computation? One sentence on the difference.

## 11. Compilers

- Compile expressions and statements down to instruction sequences over registers and a stack. Walking the expression tree in post-order produces the instruction order.
- In RTL, high-level synthesis does the same thing. Being able to picture **how many cycles one line of C costs** changes how you estimate a spec.
- Question: for `a*b + c` with one multiplier and one adder, where do you keep the intermediate result?

## 12. Procedures and Stacks

- A call saves the return address and jumps. The stack frame holds arguments, return value, locals, and the return address.
- In RTL, **the link register (r31 in Beta) and the SP update** become part of the datapath. This is the first place people get stuck building a processor.
- Question: as recursion deepens, what runs out (in both hardware and software)?

## 13. Building the Beta

- Combine a datapath (ALU, register file, memory) with control (an FSM) into one CPU. Control selects which path each instruction takes.
- In RTL, **this is the blueprint for the mini CPU in W7-W9**, and the first artifact you can honestly call "I built a CPU" in an interview.
- Question: for `ADD` and for `LW`, what is the source written into the register file in each case?

## 14. Caches and the Memory Hierarchy

- Exploit locality (temporal and spatial) by combining a small fast memory with a large slow one. Direct-mapped has one candidate slot; associative compares several. Writes are write-through or write-back.
- In RTL, **tag comparison, hit detection, and eviction (LRU)** are literally circuits. A frequent design-verification topic too.
- Question: in a direct-mapped cache, why is alternating between two addresses that share an index slow?
